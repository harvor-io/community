# Governance

Harvor uses a **benevolent dictator governance model** (sometimes called a "BDFL" — Benevolent Dictator For Life — model), a common and well-understood structure in open-source projects.

## What this means

- **Everyone is welcome to propose ideas, designs, and code.** Discussions, issues, and pull requests from any contributor are taken seriously and evaluated on their merits.
- **Day-to-day decisions are typically delegated.** Maintainers and trusted contributors are given authority over the areas they work in — code review, issue triage, roadmap details, and technical design within a given project — so that Harvor doesn't bottleneck on any one person.
- **Final direction rests with the project lead.** [John Peterson](https://github.com/john-peterson-g17) is Harvor's benevolent dictator and holds final authority over the project's direction and implementation, including the ability to override a delegated decision when necessary.

## Why this model

A single point of final authority keeps Harvor coherent as it grows: it avoids design-by-committee, resolves disagreements that don't converge on their own, and protects a consistent architectural vision across services.

In practice, this authority is used sparingly. Most decisions are made collaboratively by maintainers and contributors, through the normal review and discussion process described in [`CONTRIBUTING.md`](CONTRIBUTING.md). Overrides are reserved for cases where a decision materially affects the project's direction, consistency, or long-term health — not a substitute for the normal review process.

This model can evolve as the community and maintainer base grow. Changes to Harvor's governance would themselves go through the same open discussion process as any other significant decision.
